## What is data?

I have started a Data Science course recently, where we had a slide with content similar to this:

> Nowadays, there is more data around us than ever before. This highlights the growing importance of Data Science: understanding & analyzing this data.

While I could feel myself agreeing with this statement, I was still not quite sure I fully understand what it means. (weird that you can agree to something without understanding it in detail, but this to me seems very common with humans.)

One can rephrase the above sentence in many ways: we record more information digitally than ever before. Or we can say, more of what we do has a "footprint" in digital world. But none of this answers what **data** really is.

I set out on an investigation to find this out, & in the following I will try to summarize my findings.

### Defining Data

[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data) defines data the following way:

> Data is a collection of discrete or continuous values that **conveys information**, describing the quantity (sic), quality, fact, statistics, other basic units of meaning, or simply **sequences of symbols** that may be further interpreted formally. A datum, data value or **data point is an individual value in a collection of data**. Data is **usually organized into structures** such as tables that provide additional context and meaning, and may itself be used as data in larger structures. Data may be used as variables in a computational process. Data **may represent abstract ideas or concrete measurements**.

Notice we get to know a lot about the context surrounding data and how data behaves, but not much about what it is. "Data" is defined as a "**collection of values**" - visiting "value" only gives us the definition of "mathematical value" (in itself a very "broad term" according to the definition), which seems to be in contradiction with data possibly having qualitative nature.

Describing data, the most important thing from above seems to be that it **conveys information** (we will talk more about this later), &  in addition we got to know that
- "data" is usually made of multiple parts, which are called "data points"
- can be organized into something called a "data structure"
- may be used in a computational process
- may represent abstract ideas or something specific

All of this sounds reasonable, but we are not much closer to a definition.
